About

Academic profile

My work focuses on the relationship between political institutions, media systems, and public communication, with particular attention to Central Asia. I am especially interested in how institutional and political constraints shape substantive media pluralism, journalistic autonomy, and the adaptive strategies of independent media.

Alongside academic research, I have experience in journalism, institutional communication, applied social research, and public-facing expert communication. These experiences inform my interest in the practical conditions under which journalism, public knowledge, and political communication operate.

Research

Current research agenda

Primary agenda

Governing Media Pluralism

A comparative research agenda examining how political institutions, legal and regulatory environments, media economics, and journalistic adaptation shape media pluralism and autonomy across Central Asia.

  • Political communication and political power
  • Media governance and regulation
  • Journalistic autonomy and adaptation
  • Comparative media systems
  • Independent media sustainability

Comparative communication

Central Asia as a comparative setting

I use Central Asia not only as a regional focus, but as a comparative setting for larger questions about political competition, institutional constraint, public knowledge, and the conditions that sustain or weaken substantive media pluralism.

Academic Service

Building research infrastructure

Central Asian Journal of Family and Gender Policy (CAJFGP)

I am involved in the development of the Central Asian Journal of Family and Gender Policy, a scholarly journal initiative designed to strengthen research and academic dialogue on family and gender policy in Central Asia.

The project is being developed around questions of editorial structure, publication model, long-term sustainability, and international scholarly engagement.
