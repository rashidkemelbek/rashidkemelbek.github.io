01 / About

I study communication as an institutional and political process: who can speak, which voices remain visible, and how media systems adapt under changing political conditions.

My primary research agenda focuses on political communication and media pluralism in Central Asia, with particular attention to journalistic autonomy, media governance, independent media adaptation, and the legal and institutional conditions that shape public communication.

Alongside this core agenda, I work on interdisciplinary projects in nuclear governance and security, family and health communication, and public-policy research. My methodological work combines qualitative interviewing, documentary and institutional analysis, social network analysis, bibliometric mapping, and quantitative analysis.
